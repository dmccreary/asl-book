## 1. Stokoe's Research: The Discovery of Grammar

In the early 1960s, a hearing English professor named **William Stokoe** arrived at Gallaudet University. At the time, ASL was widely dismissed as a collection of simple pictures, gestures, or "broken English." 

Stokoe watched his Deaf colleagues and students communicating. He was struck by the speed and consistency of their movements. "This is not random gesturing," he insisted. 

Stokoe spent years analyzing signs. In **1965**, he published a groundbreaking study proving that ASL possessed its own phonology (parameters), morphology, and syntax. He defined the three parameters that build every sign: *Tab* (location), *Dez* (handshape), and *Sig* (movement). 

Stokoe's research changed the academic world, leading to the formal recognition of ASL as a natural, complete human language. Today, we build on his discoveries by exploring how ASL uses classifiers and role shifts to create visual grammar.

## 2. Classifiers and Space

**Classifiers (CL)** are handshapes that represent general categories of nouns to show location and movement.

* **CL:1**: Index finger up (single person, thin pole).
* **CL:3**: "3" handshape (vehicles).
* **CL:C**: Curved "C" handshape (cylindrical cups, bottles).

## 3. Agreement Verbs and Role Shift

* **Role Shift**: Signer shifts shoulders left/right to assume the characters of Person A and Person B during dialogue.
* **Agreement Verbs** (`GIVE`): physical path starts at subject and ends at object.
