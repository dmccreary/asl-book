## 1. Training for the Deaflympics: Action Verbs

At the track stadium, a young athlete named Jordan prepared for his sprint. Jordan was training for the **Deaflympics**, a historic international sports event for Deaf athletes. His coach, a Deaf sprinter named Marcus, stood near the starting blocks.

Marcus raised his hands and signed: `RUN` (interlocking his dominant index finger with his non-dominant thumb and wiggling them forward). 

Jordan nodded, stepped onto the track, and signed: `PRACTICE` (sliding his dominant "A" handshape back and forth across his non-dominant index finger). 

At the end of the lap, Jordan walked back, breathing heavily. Marcus smiled and signed: `SIT` (bringing his dominant "H" handshape down to land on his non-dominant "H" handshape). ## 2. Action Verbs

ASL verbs represent physical paths of movement.

* **Play**: Shaking "Y" handshapes from wrist.
* **Run**: Interlocked "L" index fingers wiggling forward.
* **Walk**: Stepping flat hands forward and back.
* **Jump**: V-finger legs bouncing off flat palm.

### Historical Spotlight: The Deaflympics
Established in **1924** in Paris, the **Deaflympics** is the first international sports event for athletes with disabilities. The games are silent—using flashing lights instead of starter pistols and visual flags instead of referee whistles.

??? question "Check Your Understanding - Click to Expand"
    **Question 1**: How do you sign 'Jump' in ASL?
    
    *Answer*: Place your non-dominant hand flat (palm up) to represent the ground. Form a "V" handshape with your dominant hand (representing legs) and bounce the fingers off your palm.
    
    **Question 2**: What is unique about visual alert systems in the Deaflympics?
    
    *Answer*: Starting lines use flashing green lights instead of starter horns, and referees wave flags instead of blowing whistles to manage play visually.
