## 1. Toby's Family Tree: The Spatial Split

Seven-year-old Toby stood in front of his class, holding a poster board covered in family photographs. Toby's parents were Deaf, and he was excited to introduce his family to his classmates using ASL.

Toby pointed to his photo of his father. He tapped the thumb of his open "5" hand shape against his **forehead** twice. `MY FATHER`, he signed. 

Then, he pointed to his photo of his mother. He tapped the same thumb of his "5" handshape against his **chin** twice. `MY MOTHER`, he signed.

One of his classmates, Leo, raised his hand. "Toby, why are those two signs so similar?" 

Toby smiled. "In sign language, there is an imaginary line split across the face," Toby explained, pointing to his nose. "Male signs are up high near your forehead, and female signs are down low near your chin. That's why father is up here, and mother is down here! It's like a secret map on your face!" Toby then showed the signs for brother and sister, showing the class how the same spatial pattern helped him build his family tree.

## 2. Core Family Vocabulary

The **Gender Line** organizes ASL family signs:
* **Male Signs** (upper face): Father, Brother, Grandfather, Uncle.
* **Female Signs** (lower face): Mother, Sister, Grandmother, Aunt.

    **Question 1**: Where is the gender line in ASL?
    
    *Answer*: The face is divided horizontally. Signs representing males are signed on the upper half of the face (forehead/temple), and signs representing females are signed on the lower half of the face (chin/jaw).
    
    **Question 2**: How is the sign for 'Grandfather' formed?
    
    *Answer*: Place the thumb of an open "5" handshape on your forehead, then bounce it forward in two small arcs (representing generations).

## 3. Social Roles and Extended Family

ASL uses distinct shapes for relationships outside the family:
* **Friend**: Hooking index fingers twice (alternating direction).
* **Uncle vs. Aunt**: initialized circular motions ('U' at forehead, 'A' at chin).
