## 1. Maya's First Deaf Chat: The Quiet Coffee Shop

On a warm Saturday afternoon, Maya walked into a local Starbucks. In the back corner, a group of about fifteen people sat around a cluster of tables. As she drew closer, she was struck by the quietness of the gathering. There was no sound of voices, yet the space was alive with dynamic movements—hands slicing through the air, faces expressing laughter, and bodies leaning forward in animated conversation. This was the weekly local **Deaf Chat**.

Maya stood at the edge of the circle, her heart racing. A friendly middle-aged woman named Elena noticed her, smiled warmly, and caught Maya's eye. Elena waved her hand in a small vertical circle to get Maya's attention.

Maya took a deep breath, brought her dominant hand to her temple, and saluted forward: `HELLO`. 

Elena's eyes widened with delight. She signed: `HELLO! NAME YOUR WHAT?` (furrowing her eyebrows).

Maya slowly fingerspelled: `M-A-Y-A`. 

Elena tapped her flat hand to her chest: `MY NAME E-L-E-N-A. NICE MEET YOU`. She signed `NICE` by sliding her dominant palm across her non-dominant palm, and `MEET` by bringing two pointing index fingers together.

Maya smiled and signed: `THANK YOU` (bringing her flat hand from her lips downward). She had realized that ASL was not just about memorizing list items; it was a bridge that connected her to a warm, welcoming community of real people.

## 2. Greetings and Politeness

Politeness is highly valued in the Deaf community. Showing gratitude and respect is essential for building connections.

### Core Greetings
* **Hello**: SALUTE from temple.
* **Goodbye**: WAVE fingers down together.
* **Good Morning**: A combination of `GOOD` (lips to palm) and `MORNING` (crook of elbow rising).

### Polite Etiquette
* **Please** (flat hand circling chest) and **Sorry** (fist circling chest). 
* **Thank You**: Flat hand from mouth forward.
* **Excuse Me**: Sliding curved hand across flat palm.

### Historical Spotlight: The Deaf Goodbye
Deaf social events are famous for the **Deaf Goodbye**. Because the Deaf community is small and highly collectivist, saying goodbye often takes an hour or more. Signers walk from table to table, exchanging final updates, planning the next meeting, and embracing multiple times. Attempting to slip out quietly (known as a "French leave") is considered impolite.

    **Question 1**: How do you sign 'Please' vs. 'Sorry'?
    
    *Answer*: Both signs are clockwise circles on the chest. However, 'Please' uses an open, flat handshape, while 'Sorry' uses a closed "S" fist handshape.
    
    **Question 2**: Why is direct eye contact considered a politeness rule in ASL?
    
    *Answer*: Because ASL is visual. Looking away from someone who is signing is equivalent to covering your ears while someone is speaking—it breaks the linguistic connection.

## 3. Affirmations and Question Grammar

In ASL, questions are defined by facial expressions, not just signs.

### Affirmations
* **Yes**: Nod head while tilting "S" handshape forward and back.
* **No**: Shake head side-to-side while tapping index/middle down to thumb.
* **Maybe**: Alternately scale flat palms up and down.
* **I Don't Know**: Touch temple, twist palm out, shrug shoulders.

### Question Forms (Facial Grammar)
* **WH-Questions** (*What, Who, Where, When, Why*): Furrow your eyebrows, tilt your head forward, and place the question sign at the end of the sentence.
* **Yes/No Questions**: Raise your eyebrows and tilt your head forward.
