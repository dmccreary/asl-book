## 1. The Milan Ban: A Tragic History

In September 1880, educators of the deaf gathered in Italy for the **Second International Congress on Education of the Deaf** (known as the **Milan Conference**). Hearing educators who favored **Oralism**—teaching deaf children to speak and lip-read while banning sign language—dominated the meeting. 

The conference passed a resolution declaring that oral education was superior and formally banned signed languages in classrooms.

The impact was devastating. Deaf teachers were dismissed from schools, and signing was suppressed. Deaf children were punished for using their hands. 

But ASL could not be destroyed. For over eighty years, Deaf students kept the language alive in secret—signing in dormitories, under bedsheets, and in school yards. In 1980, the World Federation of the Deaf formally rejected the Milan resolutions, apologizing for the harm caused. Today, ASL is celebrated in universities and schools, serving as a testament to the resilience of the Deaf community and their visual heritage.

## 2. Accessibility and Visual Technology

Visual alerts replace auditory systems in Deaf homes.

* **Flashing Lights**: Flash for doorbells, smoke alarms, baby cries.
* **Bed Shakers**: Vibrating alarms under pillows.
* **Captioning & Interpreters**: Provide visual accessibility.
