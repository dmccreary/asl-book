## 2. What is Fingerspelling?

In American Sign Language, we have unique signs for thousands of words. However, we cannot sign everything. For concepts that do not have a dedicated sign—such as people's names, titles of books, specific place names, or brand names—we use **Fingerspelling**.

Fingerspelling is the process of spelling out words letter by letter using the handshapes of the **ASL Alphabet**. It represents about 10% to 15% of natural ASL conversation. 

### Historical Spotlight: Abbe de l'Epee
In 18th-century Paris, a French priest named **Abbe de l'Epee** visited a home where he observed two deaf sisters signing to each other. Intrigued, he realized that their visual signs were a natural language. He went on to establish the first public school for the deaf in Paris, documenting manual letters and signs. His work laid the foundation for the French manual alphabet, which Laurent Clerc later brought to America to build the ASL manual alphabet.

??? question "Check Your Understanding - Click to Expand"
    **Question 1**: Is fingerspelling a substitute for signing?
    
    *Answer*: No. Fingerspelling is only used for names, titles, and English loanwords that lack ASL signs. If a concept has a dedicated sign (like `CAT` or `HAPPY`), you must sign it.
    
    **Question 2**: Why is bouncing your hand when fingerspelling a problem?
    
    *Answer*: Bouncing your hand (called "pushing") makes the letters blurry and extremely difficult to read. Keeping your hand stationary inside the fingerspelling box ensures high readability.

* **Letters A-E (Closed Handshapes)**: Focus on thumb positions (resting beside the fist in 'A', resting across palm in 'B', tucked in 'E').
* **Letters F-J (Open & Dynamic)**: The circle of 'F' (index/thumb touching) vs the tracing hook of 'J' (pinky finger swooping).
* **Letters K-O (V-shapes and Loops)**: The 'V' offset thumb of 'K' vs the flat thumb fold of 'M' and 'N'.
* **Letters P-T (Inversions and Tucks)**: The downward point of 'P' (inverted 'K') vs the thumb-tuck index of 'T'.
* **Letters U-Z (Extensions and Tracings)**: The tight fingers of 'U' vs the open fingers of 'V', and the tracing path of 'Z'.

### Double Letters
When spelling words with double letters (like *M-I-M-I* or *B-O-O-K*), remember the movement rule:
* **Contact Letters** (like *A*, *D*, *E*, *O*, *S*, *T*): Tap or bounce the handshape twice in place.
* **Non-Contact/Open Letters** (like *B*, *C*, *F*, *L*, *R*, *U*, *V*, *W*): Slide your hand slightly outward (to the right if right-handed).

## 4. ASL Numbers and Palm Orientation

ASL numbers require careful attention to the palm orientation parameter.

### Numbers 1-5: Palm Inward
When signing numbers 1-5, your palm **must face inward** (toward your face). This prevents confusion with alphabetical letters like 'D' or 'W'.
* **Number 3** is formed by extending your thumb, index, and middle fingers—not your index, middle, and ring fingers!

### Numbers 6-10: Palm Outward
When signing numbers 6-10, your palm **must face outward** (toward the receiver). You touch your thumb to different fingers (pinky for 6, ring for 7, middle for 8, index for 9).

??? question "Check Your Understanding - Click to Expand"
    **Question 1**: How does palm orientation differ between number 2 and the letter 'V'?
    
    *Answer*: For number 2, the palm faces inward (toward the signer). For the letter 'V', the palm faces outward (toward the receiver).
    
    **Question 2**: How is number 3 signed in ASL?
    
    *Answer*: It is signed with the thumb, index, and middle fingers extended. Extending the index, middle, and ring fingers is a common mistake that confuses the sign with the letter 'W'.

## 5. Age and Time Spatial Zones

ASL anchors numbers on physical body zones to express time and age.

### Age Signs (Chin Anchor)
To sign age, touch your dominant index finger to your **chin** (the age anchor), then move your hand outward while forming the number handshape. This combines "Age" and the value in a single movement.

### Time Expressions (Wrist Anchor)
To sign time, tap your index finger to your **wrist** (where a watch sits), then move your hand upward, signing the number.
