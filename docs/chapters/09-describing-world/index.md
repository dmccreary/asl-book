## 1. Painting the Playroom: Colors in Motion

Maya and her brother spent the afternoon painting a toy chest in their playroom. They had three small cans of paint lined up on newspaper. Maya dipped her brush and signed to her brother:

`LOOK. RED` (dragging her index finger down her lip). 

Her brother nodded and pointed to his can. `MY FAVORITE BLUE` (forming a "B" handshape and shaking his wrist side-to-side). 

To show the size of the toys they wanted to paint, Maya pointed to a large dollhouse. `BIG`, she signed, moving her "L" hands outward in a wide horizontal arc while widening her eyes and making a "CHA" shape with her mouth. 

Her brother pointed to a tiny toy car. `SMALL`, she signed, squeezing her palms close together and squinting her eyes. Maya realized that describing the world in ASL required combining initialized colors with physical size expressions, turning simple adjectives into a three-dimensional scale.

## 2. Colors in ASL

Many color signs are initialized, using the first letter of the color's name.

* **Color**: Wiggling fingers on chin (palm inward).
* **Red**: Drawing index finger down lip.
* **Blue**: Shaking "B" handshape.
* **Green**: Shaking "G" handshape.
* **Yellow**: Shaking "Y" handshape.

### Historical Spotlight: The Initialization Debate
In the history of ASL, **Initialization** (using English letter handshapes for signs) was introduced in schools to help teach English vocabulary. Today, there is a cultural movement to reduce initialization and prioritize traditional, root signs to preserve natural ASL structure.

??? question "Check Your Understanding - Click to Expand"
    **Question 1**: How do you sign the color 'Red'?
    
    *Answer*: Place your index finger on your lower lip and pull it downward once. This represents the redness of lips.
    
    **Question 2**: What is an initialized sign?
    
    *Answer*: A sign that uses the handshape of the first letter of the corresponding English word (e.g., 'Green' using a 'G' handshape).

## 3. Scale and Temperature

Descriptive signs use non-manual signals to convey intensity.

* **Big**: Large outward arc of "L" hands ("CHA" face).
* **Small**: Squeezing flat palms together (squinted face).
* **Hot**: Throwing curved hand from mouth out.
* **Cold**: Shivering fists at chest.
