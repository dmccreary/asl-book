## 1. The Drama Stage: Visual Volume in ASL

At the state drama festival, a Deaf actress named Clara walked onto the stage. She was performing a monologue about a character who travels through a storm of emotions. The audience, a mix of Deaf and hearing students, sat spellbound. 

Clara signed: `HAPPY`. She kept the circles small, near her chest, with a gentle, relaxed smile. 

Suddenly, her body tensed. She paused, held her hands close to her heart, and then swept them upward in large, rapid strokes, her eyes widening, her head tilting back, and a brilliant, high-intensity smile lighting up her face. She was signing `EXCITED`. 

Clara didn't use a sign for the English word "very." Instead, she changed the **speed**, **scale**, and **facial tension** of her movement. For a moment of sadness, her shoulders slumped, her hands fell slowly like heavy rain, and her mouth turned downward. By modifying the physical parameters of the signs, Clara could adjust the "volume" of her feelings, showing the difference between feeling slightly happy and wild with joy.

## 2. Core Feeling Words

Let's study the basic feeling vocabulary. Remember that facial expressions must match the sign!

* **Happy**: Flat hands brushing upward on chest in alternating circles (smiling face).
* **Sad**: Flat hands falling slowly down face (slumped shoulders, down-turned mouth).
* **Angry**: Claw hand bringing tension to face (furrowed brow).
* **Calm**: Flat hands smoothing downward and outward.

    **Question 1**: How do you sign 'very happy' in ASL without signing 'very'?
    
    *Answer*: Increase the speed and scale of the upward circular chest brushes, hold the beginning of the sign briefly for emphasis, and maximize your smiling facial expression.
    
    **Question 2**: What is a Non-Manual Signal (NMS)?
    
    *Answer*: NMS refers to grammatical movements of the face and body (like eyebrow movements, head tilts, and shoulder slumps) that accompany sign production.

## 3. High-Intensity Feelings

Intense emotions require greater muscle tension and larger movement paths.

* **Excited**: Middle fingers of open "5" hands circling chest upward.
* **Nervous**: Shivering fists or hands next to chest.
* **Scared**: Hands opening wide and crossing in front of chest.
* **Surprised**: Hands opening rapidly into circles near eyes.
